Frequently asked questions

A few useful starting points.

Every financial situation is different. These answers offer general context, not individualized advice.

What is financial planning?

Financial planning is an ongoing process for organizing your resources around your goals. It may include cash flow, investments, retirement, protection, taxes, and other decisions, depending on your needs.

Why work with a financial planner?

A planner can help you understand your current picture, evaluate options, and coordinate decisions. The value of an engagement depends on your circumstances, the services provided, and the fees involved.

How do I choose a financial planner?

Ask about qualifications, services, compensation, conflicts of interest, and how recommendations are made. Look for clear communication and a planning relationship that matches the questions you need help with.

What services can financial planning include?

Planning may cover cash flow, investments, retirement, insurance, tax considerations, estate coordination, education goals, and business planning. The scope depends on the engagement you agree to.

How do your fees work?

Investment management is a flat 1.00% of managed assets per year, billed monthly in arrears, with no asset minimum or fee tiers. Financial planning starts at $300 per month or $2,500 for a one-time plan, and is included at no additional cost once managed assets reach $300,000. À la carte advice starts at $250 per hour with a two-hour minimum. Custodian fees and internal fund expenses, if any, are separate.

Is there a minimum to work with you?

No. Investment management is available at any asset level. Financial planning can be engaged separately or included with investment management once managed assets reach $300,000.

Can I get financial planning without investment management?

Yes. Choose a one-time comprehensive plan starting at $2,500 or an ongoing planning membership starting at $300 per month.

Does financial planning include tax preparation?

No. Tax strategy may be included in financial planning, but preparing and filing tax returns are not part of the advisory relationship.

What happens on the first call?

The first conversation is free, lasts about 30 minutes, and has no obligation. We learn about your situation, answer questions, and discuss whether the services are a fit.

How do I reach my advisor?

By call, text, or email. The contact page has current phone and email details.

What should I bring to an initial conversation?

Bring the questions and goals on your mind. You do not need to have everything organized in advance. If you decide to move forward, the team can explain what information is useful for your particular planning work.
